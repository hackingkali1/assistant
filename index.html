Big Picture

The Indian Election System

India is the world's largest democracy. Here's a high-level look at how it all works.

⚖️

Election Commission of India (ECI)

An independent constitutional body that conducts free and fair elections across India. It sets dates, enforces the Model Code of Conduct, and oversees the entire process.

🏛️

Lok Sabha Elections

National Parliament elections held every 5 years. 543 constituencies, one MP elected from each. Determines who forms the Central Government.

🏢

Vidhan Sabha Elections

State Legislature elections, also held every 5 years. Determines who forms the State Government and who becomes the Chief Minister.

🏘️

Local Body Elections

Municipal corporations, panchayats, and town councils. Closest to citizens — handles local infrastructure, sanitation, and development.

💡 India uses the First Past the Post (FPTP) system — the candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins, even without a majority.

Timeline

Election Calendar

From announcement to results — a typical Lok Sabha election spans 6–8 weeks.

Week 0

📢 Election Announcement

ECI announces dates. Model Code of Conduct kicks in immediately.

Week 1–2

📝 Nomination Filing

Candidates file nominations with the Returning Officer. Scrutiny and withdrawal follows.

Week 2–6

📣 Campaign Period

Parties and candidates campaign. No campaigning allowed within 48 hrs of polling (Silence Period).

Week 3–8

🗳️ Multi-Phase Polling

Voting happens across multiple phases (up to 7) to deploy security forces efficiently. Polling time: 7 AM – 6 PM.

Week 8–9

📊 Vote Counting & Results

EVMs are counted at designated centres. Results declared, usually within the same day of counting.

Week 9–10

🏛️ Government Formation

Winning party or coalition stakes claim. President invites the leader to form the government.

Key Concepts

Understanding the Basics

Click any concept card to expand its explanation.

🏆

First Past The Post (FPTP)

+

The candidate who gets the most votes in their constituency wins — even if they don't have more than 50% of the total votes.

Example: In a 4-way race — A gets 35%, B gets 30%, C gets 20%, D gets 15%. Candidate A wins with just 35% of the votes!
🗺️

Constituencies

+

India is divided into 543 Lok Sabha constituencies. Each area elects exactly one Member of Parliament (MP) who represents that region in the national parliament.

Example: If you live in Mumbai North, you vote for one MP who represents all of Mumbai North in the Lok Sabha.
🗓️

Multi-Phase Elections

+

India is too large to vote on a single day. Polling is spread across multiple phases (typically 4–7) so that security forces can be redeployed efficiently.

Example: In the 2024 Lok Sabha election, voting happened in 7 phases between April 19 and June 1.
🔲

EVM & VVPAT

+

EVM (Electronic Voting Machine) records your vote electronically. VVPAT (Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail) prints a paper slip showing your chosen candidate for 7 seconds so you can verify it.

Why it matters: VVPAT adds a paper trail that can be audited to verify EVM results if needed.
🔢

Magic Number: 272

+

To form a majority government in the Lok Sabha, a party or coalition needs at least 272 seats (more than half of 543).

Simple math: 543 ÷ 2 = 271.5 → Round up → 272 seats needed for majority.
📜

Model Code of Conduct

+

A set of guidelines issued by ECI that all parties and candidates must follow during the election period. It prevents misuse of government power for campaign advantage.

Example rule: No new government schemes or freebies can be announced after the MCC kicks in.
After Results

How Government is Formed

Once votes are counted, here's what happens next.

1️⃣

Results Declared

The Election Commission declares results. The party or alliance with the most seats is identified.

→
2️⃣

President Invites

The President invites the leader of the party/alliance with majority support (272+ seats) to form the government.

→
3️⃣

PM Takes Oath

The Prime Minister and Cabinet are sworn in by the President at Rashtrapati Bhavan.

⚠️

What is a Hung Parliament?

When no single party wins 272+ seats, it's called a Hung Parliament. Parties then negotiate to form a coalition government. The President gives the largest party/alliance a chance to prove majority through a floor test in Parliament.

  • 📅 1996 elections led to a hung parliament
  • 📅 1999 — NDA coalition was formed
  • 📅 2004 — UPA coalition was formed
